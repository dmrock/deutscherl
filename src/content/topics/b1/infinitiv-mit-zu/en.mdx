---
title: 'Infinitiv mit zu: zu + infinitive and um … zu'
summary: After many verbs, after nouns like *Lust* or *Zeit* and after *es ist* + adjective, German puts *zu* + infinitive at the end, as in *Ich habe keine Lust, heute zu kochen*. Separable verbs take *zu* in the middle, as in *anzurufen*. Modal verbs take the infinitive without *zu*. *um … zu* says what something is for.
---

## <De>zu</De> + infinitive at the end

English often joins two verbs with "to": "I have no desire to cook". German does the same with <De>zu</De> and the infinitive, the basic form of the verb like <De>kochen</De>. Together with the words that belong to it, this is the infinitive part of the sentence.

- <De>zu</De> comes right before the infinitive.
- The infinitive is the very last word of the infinitive part.
- Everything else in the infinitive part, like <De>heute</De> or <De>nächstes Jahr</De>, comes before <De>zu</De>.
- The infinitive part has no subject of its own and never changes with the person.

A comma separates the infinitive part from the rest of the sentence. When the infinitive part is just <De>zu</De> + infinitive, with no other words, you can leave the comma out.

## After verbs, nouns and <De>es ist</De>

**1. After many verbs.** Common ones are <De>versuchen</De> (to try), <De>vergessen</De> (to forget), <De>vorhaben</De> (to plan), <De>beschließen</De> (to decide), <De>anfangen</De> (to start) and <De>aufhören</De> (to stop).

## Separable verbs

With a separable verb like <De>anrufen</De> (to call), <De>zu</De> goes between the front part and the verb, and the whole thing is written as one word: <De>anzurufen</De>, <De>mitzukommen</De>, <De>aufzustehen</De>. Verbs that never separate, like <De>besuchen</De> or <De>vergessen</De>, take <De>zu</De> in front as usual: <De>zu besuchen</De>.

## Without <De>zu</De>

The modal verbs (<De>können</De>, <De>müssen</De>, <De>wollen</De>, <De>dürfen</De>, <De>sollen</De>, <De>möchten</De>) take the infinitive without <De>zu</De>. So do <De>werden</De> in the future tense (Futur I) and <De>lassen</De>. There is also no comma before the infinitive.

## Infinitive or <De>dass</De>?

The infinitive part has no subject, so the main clause must show who does it. When the subject of the main clause does it, use <De>zu</De> + infinitive. When someone else does it, you need a clause with <De>dass</De> and its own subject.

<RuleTable equal>

| the same person | another person |
| --- | --- |
| <De>zu</De> + infinitive | <De>dass</De> + subject + verb at the end |

</RuleTable>

## <De>um … zu</De>: in order to

<De>um … zu</De> says what something is for, like "in order to" in English. <De>um</De> starts the infinitive part, and <De>zu</De> + infinitive ends it. With <De>um … zu</De>, the person who does it is the subject of the main clause.
