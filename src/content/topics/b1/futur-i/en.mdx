title: 'Futur I: werden + infinitive'
summary: For the future, German usually uses the present tense with a time word. The Futur I (*werden* + infinitive) is for plans and promises, predictions, guesses about now and firm demands. The form of *werden* is in position 2, and the infinitive goes to the end.
---

## The present tense or the Futur I?

When a time word like <De>morgen</De> (tomorrow) or <De>nächste Woche</De> (next week) shows that something is in the future, German usually uses the present tense. This is the normal way to talk about plans that are already fixed.

The Futur I adds something to the sentence: a firm intention, a promise, a prediction or a guess. So when people talk about the future, you hear the present tense more often than the Futur I.

## How the Futur I is formed

The Futur I has two parts:

1. a form of <De>werden</De> in position 2 of the sentence,
2. the infinitive of the main verb (the basic form, like <De>machen</De>) at the very end.

<RuleTable equal>

| singular | plural |
| --- | --- |
| <De plain>ich werde</De> | <De plain>wir werden</De> |
| <De plain>du **wirst**</De> | <De plain>ihr werdet</De> |
| <De plain>er / sie / es **wird**</De> | <De plain>sie / Sie werden</De> |

</RuleTable>

Only <De>werden</De> changes with the person. The infinitive at the end always stays the same. A separable verb like <De>anrufen</De> (to call) stays in one piece: <De>ich werde dich anrufen</De>.

## Word order

In a question, the order is the same as in the present tense. A yes/no question starts with the form of <De>werden</De>. A question with a question word like <De>was</De> starts with that word, and <De>werden</De> comes second. The infinitive stays at the end.

After <De>dass</De>, <De>weil</De> or <De>wenn</De>, the verb goes to the end of the clause. In the Futur I, the form of <De>werden</De> is the very last word, right after the infinitive: <De>dass sie die Stelle bekommen wird</De>.

## <De>werden</De> alone or with an infinitive

<De>werden</De> on its own is a full verb and means "to become": <De>sie wird Ärztin</De> (she is going to be a doctor). Only <De>werden</De> together with an infinitive at the end is the Futur I. With a past participle, as in <De>das Haus wird gebaut</De> (the house is being built), <De>werden</De> forms the passive (Passiv), not the Futur I.
