---
title: 'Imperativ: requests and instructions'
summary: The imperative (*Imperativ*) is the verb form for requests, instructions and advice. German has three forms, for *du*, *ihr* and *Sie*. The verb comes first, and *bitte* makes a request polite.
---

## Three forms for "you"

You use the imperative to ask or tell someone to do something. German has three words for "you", so it has three imperative forms. Each one comes from a present tense form. Only <De>sein</De> is different, see below.

<RuleTable>

| present tense | imperative |
| --- | --- |
| <De plain>du kommst</De> | <De plain>**komm**</De> |
| <De plain>ihr kommt</De> | <De plain>**kommt**</De> |
| <De plain>Sie kommen</De> | <De plain>**kommen Sie**</De> |

</RuleTable>

With <De>du</De> and <De>ihr</De>, you leave out the pronoun. <De>Sie</De> stays and comes right after the verb.

## The <De>du</De> form

Take the present tense form for <De>du</De> and drop <De>du</De> and the ending <De>-st</De>: <De>du machst</De> → <De>mach</De>, <De>du trinkst</De> → <De>trink</De>, <De>du schreibst</De> → <De>schreib</De>.

You will also see forms with <De>-e</De>, such as <De>mache</De> or <De>komme</De>. They are correct too, but this page uses the short form.

When the stem (the infinitive without <De>-en</De>) ends in <De>-t</De> or <De>-d</De>, as in <De>warten</De> (to wait), <De>arbeiten</De> (to work) or <De>finden</De> (to find), use the form with <De>-e</De>: <De>warte</De>, <De>arbeite</De>, <De>finde</De>.

## Vowel changes in the <De>du</De> form

Some verbs change their vowel in the present tense with <De>du</De>. In the imperative, it depends on the change:

<RuleTable equal>

| e → i or ie: the change stays | a → ä, au → äu: the change goes |
| --- | --- |
| <De plain>du nimmst → **nimm**</De> | <De plain>du fährst → **fahr**</De> |
| <De plain>du isst → **iss**</De> | <De plain>du schläfst → **schlaf**</De> |
| <De plain>du liest → **lies**</De> | <De plain>du läufst → **lauf**</De> |

</RuleTable>

The forms <De>du isst</De> and <De>du liest</De> end in just <De>-t</De>, so drop only the <De>-t</De>: <De>iss</De>, <De>lies</De>.

Other common verbs with e → i work the same way: <De>geben</De> → <De>gib</De>, <De>sprechen</De> → <De>sprich</De>, <De>helfen</De> → <De>hilf</De>. These forms never get <De>-e</De>, and they do not come from the infinitive: <De>nimm</De>, not <De>nehm</De>.

## The <De>ihr</De> and <De>Sie</De> forms

**1. <De>ihr</De>.** Take the present tense form for <De>ihr</De> and drop the pronoun: <De>ihr esst</De> → <De>esst</De>. The <De>ihr</De> form never changes its vowel: <De>nehmt</De>, <De>fahrt</De>.

## <De>sein</De>

<De>sein</De> (to be) has its own forms. Only <De>seid</De> is the same as in the present tense, so learn the other two by heart.

<RuleTable wide>

| <De>du</De> | <De>ihr</De> | <De>Sie</De> |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <De plain>**sei**</De> | <De plain>**seid**</De> | <De plain>**seien Sie**</De> |

</RuleTable>

With <De>sein</De> you ask someone to be a certain way: careful, quiet, on time.

## Word order: the verb comes first

In a statement, the verb is in position 2. In the imperative, it comes first.

**1. Separable verbs.** Verbs like <De>aufstehen</De> (to get up) and <De>anrufen</De> (to call) split in two, as in the present tense. The verb comes first, and the prefix (<De>auf</De>, <De>an</De>) goes to the end.
