## One verb, two parts

Many German verbs have a short front part, the prefix: <De>anrufen</De> (to call), <De>einkaufen</De> (to go shopping). In the dictionary, the verb is one word. In a simple sentence in the present tense, it splits in two. The verb part changes with the person and stands in position 2. The prefix goes to the end.

The word order topic introduces this rule briefly. This page shows which verbs split, how to recognise them, and where the prefix goes in longer sentences and in questions.

## Common prefixes

Many separable prefixes are small words you may already know, such as <De>an</De>, <De>auf</De> or <De>mit</De>.

<RuleTable>

| prefix | verbs |
| --- | --- |
| <De>an</De> | <De>anrufen</De> (to call), <De>anfangen</De> (to start), <De>ankommen</De> (to arrive) |
| <De>auf</De> | <De>aufstehen</De> (to get up), <De>aufmachen</De> (to open), <De>aufhören</De> (to stop) |
| <De>aus</De> | <De>aussteigen</De> (to get off), <De>ausmachen</De> (to switch off) |
| <De>ein</De> | <De>einkaufen</De> (to go shopping), <De>einladen</De> (to invite), <De>einsteigen</De> (to get on) |
| <De>mit</De> | <De>mitkommen</De> (to come along), <De>mitbringen</De> (to bring along) |
| <De>zu</De> | <De>zumachen</De> (to close), <De>zuhören</De> (to listen) |
| <De>ab</De> | <De>abholen</De> (to pick up), <De>abfahren</De> (to leave) |
| <De>zurück</De> | <De>zurückkommen</De> (to come back) |
| <De>fern</De> | <De>fernsehen</De> (to watch TV) |

</RuleTable>

The prefix can change the meaning of the verb completely. <De>hören</De> means "to hear", <De>aufhören</De> means "to stop" and <De>zuhören</De> means "to listen". So learn each separable verb as a whole word, with its own meaning.

<AustrianNote id="at1">
  In Austria and southern Germany, you can switch on the light with <De>aufdrehen</De>: <De>das Licht aufdrehen</De>. Everywhere, you can also say <De>einschalten</De>, or <De>anmachen</De> in informal speech. <De>aufdrehen</De> splits in the same way: the prefix <De>auf</De> goes to the end.

## The prefix goes to the very end

The prefix stays at the end, however much comes between it and the verb. Objects, times and places all go in the middle.

The same is true in questions. In a question with a question word, the verb part comes second. In a yes/no question, it comes first. In both, the prefix goes to the end.

## The verb part changes like the simple verb

The verb part has the same forms as the verb without the prefix. Some verbs change their vowel with <De>du</De> and <De>er / sie / es</De>, like <De>sehen</De> (<De>er sieht</De>) and <De>fangen</De> (<De>er fängt</De>). So <De>fernsehen</De> becomes <De>er sieht fern</De>, and <De>anfangen</De> becomes <De>er fängt an</De>. In the same way, <De>einladen</De> becomes <De>sie lädt ein</De>.

## Prefixes that stay attached: <De>be-</De>, <De>ver-</De> and others

You can hear a separable prefix: it is stressed. You say <De>anrufen</De> with the stress on <De>an</De>, and <De>aufstehen</De> with the stress on <De>auf</De>.

Verbs with <De>be-</De> or <De>ver-</De> are different. These prefixes are never stressed, and they never split. The stress is on the verb part: <De>besuchen</De> with the stress on <De>su</De>, <De>verstehen</De> with the stress on <De>ste</De>. Compare <De>ankommen</De> (to arrive) and <De>bekommen</De> (to get), or <De>aufstehen</De> (to get up) and <De>verstehen</De> (to understand).

The prefixes <De>er-</De>, <De>ent-</De> and <De>ge-</De> stay attached too: <De>erklären</De> (to explain), <De>entschuldigen</De> (to excuse), <De>gehören</De> (to belong to).
