summary: 'Some common verbs change the vowel of their stem in the present tense, but only with *du* and *er / sie / es*: *fahren*, *du fährst*, *er fährt*. The endings are the regular ones.'
---

## Only <De>du</De> and <De>er / sie / es</De> change

Most verbs keep their stem in the present tense: <De>wohnen</De>, <De>du wohnst</De>. Some common verbs change the vowel of the stem, but only in two forms: with <De>du</De> and with <De>er / sie / es</De>. This is called a vowel change (<De>Vokalwechsel</De>). All other forms keep the vowel of the infinitive, and the endings are the normal present tense endings.

| <De plain>er / sie / es **spricht**</De> | <De plain>sie / Sie sprechen</De> |

</RuleTable>

Watch out for <De>ihr</De>: it ends in <De>-t</De> like <De>er</De>, but its vowel does not change. So it is <De>ihr fahrt</De>, but <De>er fährt</De>.

## Four patterns

The vowel changes in one of four ways.

**1. e becomes i.** <De>sprechen</De> (to speak), <De>nehmen</De> (to take), <De>essen</De> (to eat), <De>geben</De> (to give), <De>helfen</De> (to help), <De>treffen</De> (to meet): <De>du hilfst</De>, <De>er gibt</De>.

## Spelling to watch

**<De>nehmen</De>.** In the changed forms the <De>h</De> goes and the <De>m</De> doubles: <De>du nimmst</De>, <De>er nimmt</De>. The other forms stay regular: <De>ich nehme</De>, <De>ihr nehmt</De>.

**Stems that end in an s sound.** <De>essen</De> and <De>lesen</De> follow the same rule as <De>heißen</De>: after an s sound, <De>du</De> adds only <De>-t</De>. So the <De>du</De> form and the <De>er</De> form are the same: <De>du isst</De>, <De>er isst</De>; <De>du liest</De>, <De>er liest</De>.

## You cannot see it in the infinitive

Many verbs with <De>e</De>, <De>a</De> or <De>au</De> in the stem stay regular. <De>gehen</De> sounds like <De>sehen</De>, but it is <De>er geht</De>. <De>kaufen</De> looks like <De>laufen</De>, but it is <De>er kauft</De>. So learn a new verb together with its <De>er</De> form: <De>fahren</De>, <De>er fährt</De>.
