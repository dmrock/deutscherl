---
title: 'Comparing: comparative and superlative'
summary: To compare two things, add *-er* to the adjective and use *als* (than), as in *schneller als*. For "the most", use *am* and *-sten*, as in *am schnellsten*. To say two things are equal, use *so … wie*. Some short adjectives get an umlaut, and a few forms are irregular, like *gut, besser, am besten*.
---

## The comparative: <De>-er</De> and <De>als</De>

To say that something is faster, smaller or older, add <De>-er</De> to the adjective. This form is the comparative (Komparativ). The thing you compare with comes after <De>als</De> (than).

## The superlative: <De>am …sten</De>

To say that something is the fastest or the most beautiful, use <De>am</De> and add <De>-sten</De> to the adjective. This form is the superlative (Superlativ).

## Irregular forms

A few common words have their own forms. Learn them by heart.

<RuleTable wide>

| adjective | comparative | superlative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <De plain>gut</De> | <De plain>**besser**</De> | <De plain>am **besten**</De> |
| <De plain>viel</De> | <De plain>**mehr**</De> | <De plain>am **meisten**</De> |
| <De plain>gern</De> | <De plain>**lieber**</De> | <De plain>am **liebsten**</De> |
| <De plain>hoch</De> | <De plain>**höher**</De> | <De plain>am **höchsten**</De> |
| <De plain>nah</De> | <De plain>**näher**</De> | <De plain>am **nächsten**</De> |

</RuleTable>

<De>gern</De> is not an adjective, but you use it to say what you like doing, so <De>lieber</De> means "prefer". <De>teuer</De> (expensive) loses an <De>e</De> in the comparative: <De>teurer</De>.
