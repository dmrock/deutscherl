summary: Nine prepositions take either the dative or the accusative. For a place, where something is (*Wo?*), use the dative. For a direction, where something goes (*Wohin?*), use the accusative.
---

## Nine prepositions, two cases

These nine prepositions are called two-way prepositions (Wechselpräpositionen):

- <De>an</De> (at, on a side), <De>auf</De> (on top of), <De>in</De> (in, into)
- <De>hinter</De> (behind), <De>vor</De> (in front of), <De>neben</De> (next to)
- <De>über</De> (above, over), <De>unter</De> (under), <De>zwischen</De> (between)

Each of them can take the dative or the accusative. Which one depends on the question the sentence answers.

## <De>Wo?</De> or <De>Wohin?</De>

<RuleTable equal>

| <De>Wo?</De> → dative | <De>Wohin?</De> → accusative |
| --- | --- |
| a place: where something is | a direction: where something goes |

</RuleTable>

## Short forms: <De>im, ins, am, ans</De>

<De>in</De> and <De>an</De> often join with <De>dem</De> or <De>das</De> into one word.

<RuleTable>

| preposition + article | short form |
| --- | --- |
| <De plain>in dem</De> | <De plain>**im**</De> |
| <De plain>in das</De> | <De plain>**ins**</De> |
| <De plain>an dem</De> | <De plain>**am**</De> |

## Putting and being somewhere

Four pairs of verbs work with these prepositions. When you put something somewhere, you answer <De>Wohin?</De> and use the accusative. When something is somewhere, you answer <De>Wo?</De> and use the dative. <De>hängen</De> has the same form in both meanings.

<RuleTable equal>

| <De>Wohin?</De> → accusative | <De>Wo?</De> → dative |
| --- | --- |
| <De>stellen</De> (to stand something up) | <De>stehen</De> (to stand) |
| <De>legen</De> (to lay something down) | <De>liegen</De> (to lie) |
| <De>sich setzen</De> (to sit down) | <De>sitzen</De> (to sit) |
| <De>hängen</De> (to hang something up) | <De>hängen</De> (to hang) |

</RuleTable>
