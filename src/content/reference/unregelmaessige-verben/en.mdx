## How to read the tables

Each table shows the infinitive (the basic form), the present tense of <De>er / sie / es</De> and the Perfekt. Verbs that form the Perfekt with <De>sein</De> show <De>ist</De> in bold; all the others use <De>haben</De>.

The Perfekt has two parts: <De>haben</De> or <De>sein</De>, and the past participle (Partizip II, like <De>gegangen</De>). Regular verbs build the participle with <De>ge-</De> and <De>-t</De> (<De>machen</De> → <De>gemacht</De>). The participles on this page end in <De>-en</De> (<De>gefahren</De>) or change their stem (<De>gedacht</De>), so learn each one with its verb.

## Vowel change in the present tense

These verbs change their vowel in the present tense, but only with <De>du</De> and <De>er / sie / es</De>: <De>ich fahre</De>, <De>du fährst</De>, <De>er fährt</De>, <De>wir fahren</De>. The other forms are regular.

Verbs with an unstressed prefix like <De>be-</De>, <De>emp-</De>, <De>ge-</De> or <De>ver-</De> get no extra <De>ge-</De> in the participle: <De>bekommen</De>, <De>empfohlen</De>, <De>gefallen</De>, <De>verstanden</De>. In <De>gehen</De>, <De>ge</De> is part of the verb, not a prefix, so the participle is <De>gegangen</De>.

<AustrianNote id="at1">

## Mixed verbs

Their participle ends in <De>-t</De> like a regular one, but the stem changes: <De>denken</De> → <De>gedacht</De>. <De>wissen</De> is also irregular in the present tense: <De>ich weiß</De>, <De>du weißt</De>, <De>er weiß</De>.

## Präteritum: sein, haben, werden and the modal verbs

In spoken German, the Perfekt is the normal past tense for most verbs. <De>sein</De>, <De>haben</De> and the modal verbs (verbs like <De>können</De> and <De>müssen</De>) are different: people use their simple past, the Präteritum, very often in speech too. <De>werden</De> is often used in the Präteritum, but the Perfekt is common in speech: <De>Ich bin krank geworden</De>. In the Präteritum, <De>ich</De> and <De>er / sie / es</De> have the same form: <De>ich war</De>, <De>er war</De>.
