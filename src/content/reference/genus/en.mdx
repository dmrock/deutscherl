---
title: 'Noun gender: clues for der, die and das'
summary: The ending of a noun or the group it belongs to often shows its gender. Endings like *-ung* give *die*, *-chen* gives *das*, and days, months and seasons take *der*. The table lists the clues, and the main exceptions follow it.
---

Most nouns give no clue, so learn them with their article. Two rules always work:

- In the plural, every noun takes <De>die</De>.
- A compound noun takes the gender of its last part: <De>der Bus</De> + <De>die Haltestelle</De> → <De>die Bushaltestelle</De>.

## Exceptions and more clues

**Feminine.** <De>-ung</De>, <De>-heit</De>, <De>-keit</De>, <De>-schaft</De>, <De>-ion</De> and <De>-tät</De> are almost always feminine. Exceptions to the other endings: <De>das Abitur</De> (school-leaving exam; in Austria <De>die Matura</De>), <De>das Knie</De> (knee), <De>der Termin</De> (appointment). With <De>-e</De>, many nouns for male people are masculine, such as <De>der Junge</De> (boy), and so are some animals, such as <De>der Löwe</De> (lion). <De>der Name</De> and <De>der Käse</De> (cheese) are masculine too, and <De>das Auge</De> (eye) and <De>das Ende</De> (end) are neuter.

**Neuter.** <De>-chen</De> always gives <De>das</De>, so <De>das Mädchen</De> (girl) is neuter. <De>Ge-</De> often gives <De>das</De>: for groups of things and for many other nouns, such as <De>das Geschenk</De> (present) and <De>das Gespräch</De> (conversation). Some are not neuter: <De>der Geschmack</De> (taste), <De>die Gefahr</De> (danger). Verbs used as nouns are neuter: <De>das Essen</De> (food, meal). Exceptions to the endings: <De>der Moment</De>, <De>die Firma</De> (company).

<AustrianNote id="at2">
Many everyday words end in <De>-erl</De>, a small-word ending like <De>-chen</De>, and many of them are neuter too: <De>das Sackerl</De> (bag), <De>das Pickerl</De> (sticker), <De>das Kipferl</De> (crescent roll). <De>Pickerl</De> is informal.
</AustrianNote>

**Masculine.** <De>-er</De> gives <De>der</De> when the noun comes from a verb and names a person or a device (<De>lehren</De> → <De>der Lehrer</De>). Other nouns with <De>-er</De> vary: <De>die Mutter</De>, <De>das Fenster</De> (window). <De>-or</De> gives <De>der</De> mainly for people and machines; exceptions are <De>das Tor</De> (gate, goal) and <De>das Labor</De> (laboratory). <De>-ent</De> for people gives <De>der</De>, but <De>-ment</De> gives <De>das</De>.

These groups are masculine too:

- days, months and seasons: <De>der Montag</De>, <De>der Mai</De>, <De>der Winter</De>, but <De>das Frühjahr</De> (spring);
- times of day: <De>der Morgen</De>, <De>der Abend</De>, but <De>die Nacht</De>;
- rain, snow and wind: <De>der Regen</De>, <De>der Schnee</De>, <De>der Wind</De>;
- male people: <De>der Mann</De>, <De>der Vater</De>.

<AustrianNote id="at1">
<De>E-Mail</De>, <De>Cola</De>, <De>Joghurt</De> and <De>Keks</De> (biscuit) are often neuter: <De>das E-Mail</De>, <De>das Cola</De>, <De>das Joghurt</De>, <De>das Keks</De>. In Germany people usually say <De>die E-Mail</De>, <De>die Cola</De>, <De>der Joghurt</De> and <De>der Keks</De>.
</AustrianNote>
